Case study · Toro Company · 2023

Lynx Mobile

Taking irrigation control into the field with a map-first experience built for crews, gloves, and dead zones.

RoleSenior Designer
ScopeMobile UX · field workflows
TeamProduct · engineering · field users
SurfaceMobile control system
The opportunity

A desktop system cannot be the only way to work in the field.

Technicians needed to diagnose faults and make changes while moving through large sites, often with gloves, carts, and unreliable signal. The product had to make the right action available without recreating desktop complexity.

The design challenge was to make a complex system feel understandable at the moment a real decision had to be made.
Double Diamond · Discover

Start with the work, not the interface.

I focused on the conditions around the interaction: location, connectivity, task urgency, and the difference between a superintendent planning and a technician responding.

Context

The field is not a smaller desktop.

Mobile had to be designed around movement, context, and resilient feedback.

Research lens

Fast feedback matters more than feature density.

Observation, stakeholder alignment, and prototype feedback kept the work grounded in real constraints.

Double Diamond · Define

Turn complexity into a usable point of view.

We framed the product around map context, action confidence, and offline resilience—three principles that shaped the information architecture and interaction model.

01 · Discover

Understand people, constraints, hardware, data, and context.

02 · Define

Make the problem and decision model legible to the team.

03 · Develop

Prototype flows, states, and edge cases across surfaces.

04 · Deliver

Double Diamond · Develop

Make the map useful at the point of action.

The work brought controllers, zones, alerts, and next actions into a field-ready spatial interface.

Map-first control makes the relationship between place, system state, and action easier to understand.

Double Diamond · Deliver

A companion that can stand on its own.

The result was a mobile experience that supports daily field work without asking users to think like the desktop system.

What changed

Less driving back to the desk.

Critical actions and feedback are available where the work happens.

What scales

Patterns over one-off screens.

Reusable interaction and content structures create leverage across the wider product ecosystem.
